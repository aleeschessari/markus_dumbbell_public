\title{The bubble on the dumbbell as a quantics tensor network}

\begin{abstract}
Given a Green function $G_q$ on a dumbbell contour $C_\lambda$, we compute the bubble $\chi_Q$ on a second dumbbell $C_\mu$, for all transfer momenta at once, as a quantics tensor network. The exterior Cauchy formula gives $G$ at the shifted points from its values on $C_\lambda$, so no analytic continuation is needed. In Fourier-mode space the geometry enters only through a three-index kernel that does not depend on $G$; weighted and compressed by a truncated HOSVD at $10^{-8}$, it has ranks $(31,33,44)$. The contraction takes about 67\,s, or 35\,s if the second of its two branches is obtained by reflecting the first, and its result agrees with the Lindhard function to $1.7\times10^{-7}$ of its maximum. We derive every formula we use. All numbers are for $\lambda=0.2$, $\mu=0.4$, $\beta=\pi$, $N=512$ contour points, $N_q=1024$ momenta and $G_q(z)=1/(z-3\cos q)$.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{The problem}
\label{sec:problem}

The input is $G_q$ on the dumbbell $C_\lambda$, at the nodes $z_j=\omega_\lambda(\phi_j)$, $\phi_j=-1+8j/N$, $j=0,\ldots,N-1$, for the momenta $q=2\pi m/N_q$; it may be given as a quantics tensor train (QTT, Sec.~\ref{sec:qtt}). The output is the bubble $\chi_Q$ for all transfer momenta $Q$, on the dumbbell $C_\mu$ of $\chi$, at the points $w_k=\omega_\mu(\phi_k)$; here $\omega_\mu$ is the doubled map, around the band $[-6,6]$ of $\chi$. The method must work without analytic continuation and without evaluating $G$ away from $C_\lambda$, at a cost that does not depend on $N$. The maps $\omega_\lambda$, $\omega_\mu$ and the levels $\lambda$, $\mu$ are defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:dumbbell}.

  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{bubble_tn_dumbbells.pdf}
  \caption{(a) The contour of $G$ ($\lambda=0.2$, blue) encloses the cut $[-3,3]$ and leaves out the poles of the Fermi function at $\pm i,\pm3i$. The contour of $\chi$ ($\mu=0.4$, purple) encloses the cut $[-6,6]$ of $\chi$ and leaves out the bosonic frequencies $\pm2i,\pm4i$.
  (b) The bubble needs $G$ at the shifted points $z_j\pm w_k$, i.e.\ on the copies $C_\lambda\pm w_k$, drawn here for three points $w_k$ (stars) on $C_\mu$ (purple). The lowest dumbbell level reached by the copies is $\lambda'_{\min}=0.237>\lambda=0.2$.}
  \label{fig:dumbbells}

The bubble needs $G$ at the shifted points $z_j\pm w_k$, which lie on the copies $C_\lambda\pm w_k$ of the contour [Fig.~\ref{fig:dumbbells}(b)]. The two contours must be chosen so that these copies never touch the cut of $G$, which makes the contour formula equal to $\chi$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:contour}), and stay outside $C_\lambda$, so that $G$ there follows exactly from $G$ on $C_\lambda$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:cauchy}). In general the second condition requires $\lambda\lesssim\mu/2$. In the example the lowest dumbbell level reached by the copies is $\lambda'_{\min}=0.237$, above $\lambda=0.2$.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

All numerical results are for a tight-binding chain with nearest-neighbour hopping,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \varepsilon_q=3\cos q,\qquad G_q(z)=\frac1{z-\varepsilon_q},\\
  A_q(\varepsilon)=\delta(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_q),
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
on the momentum grid $q=2\pi m/N_q$, $m=0,\ldots,N_q-1$, with $N_q=1024$, and at inverse temperature $\beta=\pi$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:dumbbell} gives the reason for this value). The band is $[-3,3]$, and the bubble has its cut on $[-6,6]$. $G$ is stored on the dumbbell $C_\lambda$ with $\lambda=0.2$ at $N=512$ nodes, and $\chi$ is computed at the $N=512$ points of $C_\mu$ with $\mu=0.4$; in bits, the momenta take $R_q=10$ and the contour points $R=9$. For this model the bubble is the Lindhard function \eqref{eq:lindhard}, the reference for every accuracy quoted. The method itself uses only the analyticity of $G_q$ off the band and its decay like $1/z$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:cauchy}), so interacting Green functions with their spectrum in $[-3,3]$ are treated in the same way.

\section{The formula behind the network}
\label{sec:formula}

\subsection{The dumbbell family}
\label{sec:dumbbell}

\paragraph{The map.} Let $\kappa=3/\sqrt{10}$, $\kappa'=\sqrt{1-\kappa^2}=1/\sqrt{10}$, and let $K\equiv K(\kappa)$ and $K'\equiv K(\kappa')$ be the complete elliptic integrals of the first kind with parameter $m=\kappa^2=0.9$ and $m'=0.1$: $K=2.5781$, $K'=1.6124$. With the Jacobi function $\sd=\sn/\dn$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \omega(\zeta)=\kappa\,\sd\!\Big(\frac K2(\zeta+1)\;\Big|\;\kappa^2\Big),\qquad \zeta=\phi+i\tau\lambda,\\
  \tau=\frac{2K'}{K}=1.2509,\qquad \alpha=\frac{\pi\tau}4=0.9824 .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
We write $\omega_\lambda(\phi)=\omega(\phi+i\tau\lambda)$: $\phi$ is the position along a contour and $\lambda$ its \emph{level}. ($\tau$ equals $K'(k)/K(k)$ for the modulus $k=(L-1)/(L+1)$, $L=\sqrt{10}$, which is the descending Landen transform of $\kappa$.) The function $\sd$ satisfies $\sd(u+2K)=-\sd(u)$, $\sd(u+2iK')=-\sd(u)$ and $\sd(-u)=-\sd(u)$, and its Taylor coefficients are real, so
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \omega(\zeta+8)=\omega(\zeta),\qquad \omega(\zeta+4)=-\omega(\zeta),\\
  \omega(2-\zeta)=\omega(\zeta),\qquad \overline{\omega(\zeta)}=\omega(\bar\zeta)=\omega(2-\bar\zeta).
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:symmetries}
\end{equation}
In terms of $\phi$ and $\lambda$: period 8 in $\phi$; $\omega_\lambda(\phi+4)=-\omega_\lambda(\phi)$; and $\omega_\lambda(2-\phi)=\overline{\omega_\lambda(\phi)}$, so the lower half of every contour is the mirror image of its upper half.

\paragraph{The contours.} For fixed $\lambda\in(0,1)$, $\phi\mapsto\omega_\lambda(\phi)$, $\phi\in[-1,7)$, traces a closed curve $C_\lambda$ once, the \emph{dumbbell at level $\lambda$} [Fig.~\ref{fig:dumbbells}(a)]. Its shape follows from the values at $\lambda\to0$ and $\lambda=1$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\lambda\to0^+$: $\omega$ is real and runs $0\to3\to0\to-3\to0$ for $\phi=-1\to1\to3\to5\to7$. The contour collapses onto the band $[-3,3]$: $\phi\in(-1,1)\cup(5,7)$ is the upper lip ($\Imag\omega>0$), $\phi\in(1,5)$ the lower lip. The \emph{tips} are $\phi=1$ and $\phi=5$ ($\omega=\pm3$), the \emph{pinches} $\phi=-1\equiv7$ and $\phi=3$ ($\omega=0$). In increasing $\phi$ the curve is traversed clockwise.
  \item $\lambda=1$: $\omega(\phi+i\tau)$ is purely imaginary with $\lvert\Imag\omega\rvert\ge1$ ($\omega(-1+i\tau)=i$, and $\omega\to\infty$ at $\phi=1,5$, where $\sd$ has its poles). The level-1 ``contour'' is the two rays $\{\Real z=0,\ \lvert\Imag z\rvert\ge1\}$.
\end{itemize}
Every level $0<\lambda<1$ lies between the band and the rays. The strip $0<\lambda<2$ is mapped by $\omega$ onto $\mathbb C\setminus[-3,3]$ (the level $2-\lambda$ gives the mirror image $-\overline{C_\lambda}$ of $C_\lambda$, the same curve), and $\lambda=2$ is the band again.

\paragraph{Why $\beta=\pi$.} The Fermi function $f(z)=1/(e^{\beta z}+1)$ has its poles at the fermionic Matsubara frequencies $i\omega_n=i(2n+1)\pi/\beta$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:bubble}). The two closest to the real axis, $\pm i\pi/\beta$, sit at the ends $\pm i$ of the level-1 rays exactly when $\pi/\beta=1$, i.e.\ $\beta=\pi$, and then all poles lie on the rays. This is the choice of modulus of Lin, Lu, Ying and E, $L=\sqrt{1+(E\beta/\pi)^2}=\sqrt{10}$ for $E=3$; equivalently $\kappa/\kappa'=E\beta/\pi=3$. A different temperature needs a different $\kappa$ (then $\kappa^2=a^2/(1+a^2)$, $a=E\beta/\pi$, and the prefactor $\kappa$ in \eqref{eq:map} becomes $E\kappa'$); nothing else changes. Every contour $C_\lambda$ with $0<\lambda<1$ separates the band of $G$ from all poles of $f$.

\paragraph{Jacobian.} With $v=\frac K2(\zeta+1)$ and $\frac{d}{dv}\sd v=\cn v/\dn^2v$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  J(\zeta)\equiv\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\phi}=\kappa\,\frac K2\,\frac{\cn v}{\dn^2v},\qquad
  \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\lambda}=i\tau\,J,\\
  J^2=\Big(\frac{K\kappa'}2\Big)^2(1+\omega^2)(9-\omega^2),
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
the second relation because $\omega$ is analytic in $\zeta=\phi+i\tau\lambda$, the third from $\dn^2v=1/(1+\omega^2)$ and $\cn^2v=\kappa'^2(9-\omega^2)/[\kappa^2(1+\omega^2)]$. We write $J_\lambda(\phi)=J(\phi+i\tau\lambda)$. For $0<\lambda<1$, $J_\lambda$ has no zeros on $C_\lambda$ ($\lvert J\rvert\in[0.64,2.08]$ at $\lambda=0.1$); on the band it is $J_0(\varepsilon)=\frac{K\kappa'}2\sqrt{(9-\varepsilon^2)(1+\varepsilon^2)}$, which vanishes like $\sqrt{3-\lvert\varepsilon\rvert}$ at the edges, so the nodes cluster at the band edges like Chebyshev points.

\paragraph{The dumbbell of $\chi$.} The bubble $\chi_Q$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:bubble}) has its cut on $[-6,6]$ and its bosonic frequencies $i\nu_m=2\pi im/\beta=2im$ at $\lvert\Imag w\rvert\ge2$. Both are twice the corresponding sets of $G$, so the contour of $\chi$ at level $\mu$ is the doubled map
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  w=\omega_\mu(\phi)\equiv2\,\omega(\phi+i\tau\mu),\\
  w_k=\omega_\mu(\phi_k),\qquad w_{k+N/2}=-w_k ,
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:doubled}
\end{equation}
with the same $\tau$ and $\alpha$; the last relation is $\omega(\zeta+4)=-\omega(\zeta)$ with $\phi_{k+N/2}=\phi_k+4$. For $0<\mu<1$, $C_\mu$ encloses $[-6,6]$ and leaves out all $i\nu_m$, $m\ne0$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:dumbbells}(a)].

\paragraph{Dumbbell coordinates of a point.} Every $z\in\mathbb C\setminus[-3,3]$ off the rays is $\omega(\zeta)$ for exactly one $\zeta=\phi+i\tau\lambda'$ with $\phi\in[-1,7)$, $\lambda'\in(0,1)$; on the rays the two parameter values with $\lambda'=1$ are identified. We call $\lambda(z)\equiv\lambda'$ the \emph{level} of $z$: the level of the dumbbell through $z$. Inverting \eqref{eq:map} with $\sn v=z/(\kappa\sqrt{1+z^2})$ gives the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \Zc_0(z)=\frac2K\,F\!\Big(\arcsin\frac{z}{\kappa\sqrt{1+z^2}}\;\Big|\;\kappa^2\Big)-1
  \label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
($F$ the incomplete elliptic integral of the first kind, principal branches). $\Zc_0$ returns $\Real\Zc_0\in(-3,1)$ and $\Imag\Zc_0/\tau\in(-1,1)$, and the representative in the fundamental domain is $\Zc(z)=\Zc_0(z)$ if $\Imag\Zc_0\ge0$, $\Zc(z)=2-\Zc_0(z)$ otherwise (by $\omega(2-\zeta)=\omega(\zeta)$), followed by $\Real\Zc\to((\Real\Zc+1)\bmod8)-1$. Newton's method on $\omega(\zeta)=z$ with the Jacobian \eqref{eq:jacobian} gives the same result. The inverse map is only needed for the geometry below; the method itself never uses it.

\paragraph{The copies and $\lambda'_{\min}$.} The bubble needs $G$ at the shifted points $z_j\pm w_k$ [Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrature}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:direct}], which lie on the translated copies $C_\lambda\pm w_k$ of the contour [Fig.~\ref{fig:dumbbells}(b)]. Each shifted point has a level; the lowest one,
\begin{equation}
  \lambda'_{\min}(\lambda,\mu)=\min_{j,k}\ \lambda\big(z_j\pm w_k\big),
  \label{eq:lammin}
\end{equation}
measures how close the copies come to the band ($\lambda'_{\min}>0$) and whether they stay outside $C_\lambda$ ($\lambda'_{\min}>\lambda$). By the symmetry $w_{k+N/2}=-w_k$ the sets $\{z_j+w_k\}$ and $\{z_j-w_k\}$ coincide. For the example $\lambda'_{\min}(0.2,0.4)=0.237$; for $(\lambda,\mu)=(0.1,0.2)$ it is $0.134$. The copies come closest to the band when $w_k$ is near a pinch of $C_\mu$ and $z_j$ near a tip of $C_\lambda$. Raising $\mu$ or lowering $\lambda$ raises $\lambda'_{\min}$, and $\lambda'_{\min}>\lambda$ holds for $\mu$ above a threshold $\mu_{\rm fwd}(\lambda)$, which is $0.17$, $0.36$ and $0.62$ for $\lambda=0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.3$: roughly for $\lambda\lesssim\mu/2$.

\subsection{The bubble}
\label{sec:bubble}

Momenta are on the grid $q=2\pi m/N_q$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:model}, and all momentum sums and shifts are modulo $2\pi$. The temperature is $1/\beta$; the fermionic and bosonic Matsubara frequencies are
\begin{equation}
  \omega_n=\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{\beta},\qquad \nu_m=\frac{2\pi m}{\beta},\qquad n,m\in\mathbb Z,
  \label{eq:matsubara}
\end{equation}
and the Fermi function is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  f(z)=\frac1{e^{\beta z}+1},\qquad f(z+i\nu_m)=f(z),\\
  \Res_{z=i\omega_n}f=-\frac1\beta .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:fermi}
\end{equation}
The one-particle Green function $G_q(z)$ is analytic off the real axis and has its spectrum in the band $[-E,E]$ with $E=3$. In terms of its spectral function $A_q$, supported in $[-3,3]$,
\begin{equation}
  G_q(z)=\int_{-3}^{3}d\varepsilon\,\frac{A_q(\varepsilon)}{z-\varepsilon},\qquad G_q(z)\sim\frac1z\ \ (z\to\infty).
  \label{eq:lehmann}
\end{equation}
We define the bubble without a minus sign and with the average over the internal momentum,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \chi_Q(i\nu_m)={}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\frac1\beta\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}G_q(i\omega_n)\\
  &\times G_{q+Q}(i\omega_n+i\nu_m)
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:bubble_def}
\end{equation}
The Matsubara sum converges absolutely because the summand is $O(\omega_n^{-2})$.

\paragraph{The sum as residues.} The Fermi function turns every term of the Matsubara sum into a residue. It has a simple pole wherever $e^{\beta z}=-1$, i.e.\ exactly at $z=i\omega_n$. Near such a point $e^{\beta z}=-e^{\beta(z-i\omega_n)}$, so $e^{\beta z}+1=1-e^{\beta(z-i\omega_n)}\approx-\beta(z-i\omega_n)$ and $f(z)\approx-1/[\beta(z-i\omega_n)]$: the residue is $-1/\beta$, as in \eqref{eq:fermi}. For any $h$ that is regular at the points $i\omega_n$, $\Res_{z=i\omega_n}[f(z)h(z)]=h(i\omega_n)\Res_{z=i\omega_n}f=-h(i\omega_n)/\beta$, hence
\begin{equation}
  \frac1\beta\sum_nh(i\omega_n)=-\sum_n\Res_{z=i\omega_n}\big[f(z)\,h(z)\big].
  \label{eq:matsum_residues}
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:contour} evaluates the right-hand side with a contour integral, for any $G$. For the model \eqref{eq:model} it can also be summed in closed form, which gives the Lindhard function, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lindhard} of Appendix~\ref{app:lindhard}. All accuracies quoted below are comparisons with \eqref{eq:lindhard} at $w=w_k$.

\subsection{From the Matsubara sum to the contour formula}
\label{sec:contour}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{contours_C.pdf}
  \caption{The contours of Sec.~\ref{sec:contour} in the complex $z$-plane, for $\beta=\pi$ (poles of $f$ at $\pm i,\pm3i$) and $m=1$ ($\nu_1=2$). The cut of $G_q(z)$ is the band; the cut of $G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)$ is the band moved down by $\nu_m$. The contour $C$, drawn as the dumbbell $C_{0.2}$, encloses the first cut, its copy $C-i\nu_m$ the second, and the large circle $C_R$ of the residue argument encloses both and the poles of $f$. All contours run counterclockwise.}
  \label{fig:contours}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The sum as a contour integral.} Fix $q$, $Q$ and $m\neq0$, and put $h(z)=G_q(z)\,G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)$. In the complex $z$-plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:contours}), $G_q(z)$ has its cut on the band $[-3,3]$ by \eqref{eq:lehmann}, and $G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)$ has its cut where $z+i\nu_m\in[-3,3]$, that is on the segment $[-3,3]-i\nu_m=\{x-i\nu_m:\ -3\le x\le3\}$, a copy of the band moved down by $\nu_m$. Elsewhere $h$ is analytic, and $h=O(z^{-2})$ at infinity. The poles of $f$, at $i\omega_n=i(2n+1)\pi/\beta$, sit on the imaginary axis at odd multiples of $i\pi/\beta$, and the two cuts at the heights $0$ and $-\nu_m$, which are even multiples, so no pole lies on a cut. For $m=0$ the two cuts would coincide; that case is not needed here.

Two closed contours enter, both traversed counterclockwise:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $C$ encloses the band $[-3,3]$ and nothing else: no pole of $f$ and no point of the second cut.
  \item $C-i\nu_m=\{z-i\nu_m:\ z\in C\}$ is the same contour moved down by $\nu_m$. It encloses the second cut and, like $C$, no pole of $f$, because a shift by $i\nu_m$, an even multiple of $i\pi/\beta$, maps the set of poles onto itself.
\end{itemize}
Because $C-i\nu_m$ is an exact copy of $C$, the second integral can later be moved onto $C$.

By \eqref{eq:matsum_residues}, the Matsubara sum is minus the sum of the residues of $fh$ at the poles of $f$. Let $C_R$ be the circle $\lvert z\rvert=R$, also counterclockwise, with $R$ halfway between two poles of $f$. Inside $C_R$, $fh$ is analytic except at the poles of $f$ and on the two cuts, so by the residue theorem
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_R}f\,h\,dz={}&\sum_{\lvert\omega_n\rvert<R}\Res_{z=i\omega_n}(fh)\\
  &+\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_Cf\,h\,dz\\
  &+\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C-i\nu_m}f\,h\,dz .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:residues_cuts}
\end{equation}
As $R\to\infty$ the left-hand side vanishes, because $h=O(z^{-2})$ and $f$ stays bounded on $C_R$, and the sum runs over all $n$. Then \eqref{eq:residues_cuts} says that the residues at the poles of $f$ add up to minus the two contour integrals, and \eqref{eq:matsum_residues} turns this into
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \frac1\beta\sum_nh(i\omega_n)={}&\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_Cf\,h\,dz\\
  &+\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C-i\nu_m}f\,h\,dz .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:two_cuts}
\end{equation}
For the dumbbell $C_\lambda$ the poles are always outside, since they lie on the level-1 rays. The second cut is outside when the dumbbell is not too large: $C_{0.2}$ stays within $\lvert\mathrm{Im}\,z\rvert\le0.53$, while the second cut lies at the height $-\nu_m$, with $\lvert\nu_m\rvert\ge2\pi/\beta=2$. At larger levels the dumbbell grows ($\lvert\mathrm{Im}\,z\rvert$ up to 1.6 at $\lambda=0.5$ and 4.8 at $\lambda=0.8$) and can reach the second cut. This is condition (ii) below, at $w=i\nu_m$.

\paragraph{Moving the second cut onto the first.} In the second integral of \eqref{eq:two_cuts}, write the points of $C-i\nu_m$ as $z-i\nu_m$ with $z$ on $C$, and use $f(z-i\nu_m)=f(z)$ from \eqref{eq:fermi}:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\oint_{C-i\nu_m}f(z)\,G_q(z)\,G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)\,dz\\
  &\quad=\oint_Cf(z)\,G_q(z-i\nu_m)\,G_{q+Q}(z)\,dz .
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In this integral, now over $C$, relabel $q\to q-Q$ in the momentum average, which turns $G_q(z-i\nu_m)G_{q+Q}(z)$ into $G_{q-Q}(z-i\nu_m)G_q(z)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \chi_Q(i\nu_m)={}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_Cdz\;f(z)\,G_q(z)\\
  &\times\Big[G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)+G_{q-Q}(z-i\nu_m)\Big].
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:contour_matsubara}
\end{equation}
Only one contour is left, and $f$ is evaluated only on it. Inside $C$ the integrand has only the cut of $G_q(z)$; the cuts of $G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)$ and $G_{q-Q}(z-i\nu_m)$, at $[-3,3]-i\nu_m$ and $[-3,3]+i\nu_m$, are outside.

\paragraph{Continuation to a complex frequency.} For $w\notin\mathbb R$ define
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  X_Q(w)={}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_Cdz\;f(z)\,G_q(z)\\
  &\times\Big[G_{q+Q}(z+w)+G_{q-Q}(z-w)\Big],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:XQ}
\end{equation}
with $C$ enclosing $[-3,3]$ and no pole of $f$, and leaving the shifted cuts $[-3,3]\mp w$ outside. Insert \eqref{eq:lehmann} for both Green functions and do the $z$-integral by residues. In the first term the integrand $f(z)/[(z-\varepsilon)(z+w-\varepsilon')]$ has only the pole $z=\varepsilon$ inside $C$ ($z=\varepsilon'-w$ is on the shifted cut, outside), with residue $f(\varepsilon)/(w+\varepsilon-\varepsilon')$. In the second term the pole $z=\varepsilon$ gives $f(\varepsilon)/(\varepsilon-w-\varepsilon')$; relabelling $q\to q+Q$ and exchanging $\varepsilon\leftrightarrow\varepsilon'$ turns it into $-f(\varepsilon')/(w+\varepsilon-\varepsilon')$ multiplying $A_q(\varepsilon)A_{q+Q}(\varepsilon')$. Together,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  X_Q(w)={}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\int\!\!\int d\varepsilon\,d\varepsilon'\,A_q(\varepsilon)\,A_{q+Q}(\varepsilon')\\
  &\times\frac{f(\varepsilon)-f(\varepsilon')}{w+\varepsilon-\varepsilon'} .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
This is the spectral (Lehmann) representation of the bubble. At $w=i\nu_m$ it equals \eqref{eq:contour_matsubara}, i.e.\ $\chi_Q(i\nu_m)$. Since it is also analytic in $w$ off the real axis and decays like $1/w$, it is the analytic continuation of $\chi_Q$ to complex frequencies, and \eqref{eq:XQ} evaluates it exactly at any such $w$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \chi_Q(w)={}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_Cdz\;f(z)\,G_q(z)\\
  &\times\Big[G_{q+Q}(z+w)+G_{q-Q}(z-w)\Big]
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:contour_w}
\end{equation}
for every $w\notin\mathbb R$ and every counterclockwise $C$ that (i) encloses $[-3,3]$ and no pole of $f$ and (ii) leaves out the shifted cuts $[-3,3]\mp w$. With the contour $C_\lambda$ and $w=w_k\in C_\mu$, (i) is $0<\lambda<1$, and (ii) says that the copies $C_\lambda\pm w_k$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:dumbbells}(b) do not meet the band: $\lambda'_{\min}>0$ in \eqref{eq:lammin}. If (ii) fails, the contour picks up part of a shifted cut and the result is wrong at every $N$.

\subsection{The dumbbell quadrature}
\label{sec:quadrature}

\paragraph{Weights.} Parametrize $C_\lambda$ by $\phi$: $z=\omega_\lambda(\phi)$, $dz=J_\lambda(\phi)\,d\phi$. The parametrization is clockwise, so for any $F$ analytic near the contour
\begin{equation}
  \frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\lambda}F(z)\,dz=-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{-1}^{7}d\phi\;J_\lambda(\phi)\,F\big(\omega_\lambda(\phi)\big).
\end{equation}
The integrand is 8-periodic in $\phi$, and the trapezoidal rule on the uniform grid $\phi_j$ is $\Delta\phi\sum_j$, $\Delta\phi=8/N$. With the Fermi function in the integrand it gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\lambda}f\,F\,dz\approx\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_j\,F(z_j),\\
  c_j=d_j\,f(z_j),\qquad d_j=-\frac{\Delta\phi}{2\pi i}J_\lambda(\phi_j).
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
For every $F$ that is analytic outside $C_\lambda$ and $O(z^{-2})$ at infinity, $\sum_jc_jF(z_j)\approx\beta^{-1}\sum_nF(i\omega_n)$: the $N$ nodes with the weights $c_j$ replace the Matsubara frequencies. As a check of \eqref{eq:weights}, $\sum_jc_jG_q(z_j)=f(\varepsilon_q)$ to $4\times10^{-15}$ at $\lambda=0.1$, $N=2^{10}$. Applied to \eqref{eq:contour_w}, the weights give the \emph{direct dumbbell formula},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \chi_Q(w)\approx{}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\sum_jc_j\,G_q(z_j)\\
  &\times\Big[G_{q+Q}(z_j+w)+G_{q-Q}(z_j-w)\Big],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:direct}
\end{equation}
which still needs $G$ at the shifted points $z_j\pm w$, off the contour. The $c_j$ depend on $j$, $\lambda$ and $\beta$ only, never on $w$, $q$ or $G$, and the Fermi function is evaluated only at the nodes of $C_\lambda$. Both sets of weights are smooth in $\phi_j$ (QTT rank 6 to 8). The quadrature error falls exponentially with $N$, like $e^{-\alpha Nd}$ \eqref{eq:rate}, where $d$ is the distance in level from $C_\lambda$ to the nearest singularity of the integrand (Appendix~\ref{app:trap}).

\subsection{The exterior Cauchy formula}
\label{sec:cauchy}

\paragraph{$G$ off the contour from $G$ on the contour.} Every Green function whose spectrum lies inside $C_\lambda$, interacting ones included, is analytic outside $C_\lambda$ and vanishes like $1/x$ at infinity. Let $x$ be outside $C_\lambda$ and apply Cauchy's integral formula to the region between $C_\lambda$ and a circle $C_R$, $R\to\infty$, both counterclockwise:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  G(x)&=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_R}\frac{G(s)}{s-x}\,ds-\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\lambda}\frac{G(s)}{s-x}\,ds\\
  &\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}\;\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\lambda}\frac{G(s)}{x-s}\,ds ,
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
because the $C_R$ integral is $O(1/R)$. The trapezoidal rule, $\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\lambda}F\,dz\approx\sum_md_m\,F(z_m)$ with the weights $d_m$ of \eqref{eq:weights}, turns this into a sum over the nodes:
\begin{equation}
  G(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}d_m\,\frac{G(z_m)}{x-z_m}\qquad(x\ \text{outside }C_\lambda),
  \label{eq:ext_cauchy}
\end{equation}
up to the trapezoidal error. For $x$ \emph{inside} $C_\lambda$ the same sum gives $0$ instead of $G(x)$: then $s\mapsto G(s)/(x-s)$ is analytic outside $C_\lambda$ and $O(s^{-2})$, so its integral over $C_\lambda$ equals its integral over $C_R$, which vanishes. The formula therefore applies only to shifted points outside the contour. Its convergence rate is \eqref{eq:rate} with $d=\min(\lambda,\lambda(x)-\lambda)$, because the integrand $G(\omega(\zeta))/(x-\omega(\zeta))$ has a pole at the level $\lambda(x)$ of $x$. The Cauchy kernel $1/(x-z_m)$ is bounded by the inverse distance of $x$ from the contour, so an error $\delta$ in the stored values of $G$ changes $G(x)$ by at most $\delta\sum_m\lvert d_m\rvert$ divided by that distance.

\paragraph{The exterior-Cauchy form of the bubble.} Put $x=z_j\pm w_k$ in \eqref{eq:ext_cauchy} and insert it into \eqref{eq:direct}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_Q(w_k)={}&\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\sum_{j,m}c_j\,G_q(z_j)\,d_m\\
&\times\left[\frac{G_{q+Q}(z_m)}{z_j-z_m+w_k}+\frac{G_{q-Q}(z_m)}{z_j-z_m-w_k}\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cauchy_form}
\end{equation}
Only the values of $G$ at the nodes of the stored contour enter. The formula needs no inverse map, no continuation and no mode cutoff, and its kernel $1/(z_j-z_m\pm w_k)$ is explicit and independent of $G$. In the continuum it is the double contour integral $\frac1{(2\pi i)^2}\oint\!\oint f(z)G_q(z)G_{q\pm Q}(s)/(z-s\pm w)\,dz\,ds$. Uncompressed, \eqref{eq:cauchy_form} agrees with the Lindhard sum \eqref{eq:lindhard} to $2.1\times10^{-12}$ for $(\lambda,\mu)=(0.2,0.4)$ at $N=512$ and to $1.1\times10^{-13}$ for $(0.1,0.2)$ at $N=1024$. At $N=256$ for $(0.2,0.4)$ the error is $2.1\times10^{-8}$, and the ratio of the two errors is the $e^{-\alpha N(\lambda'_{\min}-\lambda)}$ of \eqref{eq:rate}.

\paragraph{The three conditions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $0<\lambda<1$: $C_\lambda$ leaves out the poles of $f$.
  \item $\lambda'_{\min}>0$: the copies $C_\lambda\pm w_k$ avoid the band, so the contour formula \eqref{eq:contour_w} is $\chi$.
  \item $\lambda'_{\min}>\lambda$: the shifted points lie outside $C_\lambda$, so the exterior Cauchy formula \eqref{eq:ext_cauchy} applies to them (the \emph{forward-safe} condition, roughly $\lambda\lesssim\mu/2$).
\end{enumerate}
The quadrature error of \eqref{eq:cauchy_form} is exponential in $N$, with the rate set by the smaller of $\lambda$ and $\lambda'_{\min}-\lambda$. For a smaller $\mu$ than the forward-safe condition allows, the same formula works with the $m$-sum taken on a second, lower contour $C_{\lambda_2}$, $\lambda_2<\lambda'_{\min}$.

\subsection{Mode space}
\label{sec:modes}

\paragraph{Fourier coefficients on the two dumbbells.} A function given on the $N$ nodes of $C_\lambda$ has the discrete Fourier representation
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  G_q(z_j)&=\sum_{n}\hat g_{q,n}\,e^{i\pi n\phi_j/4},\\
  \hat g_{q,n}&=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}G_q(z_j)\,e^{-i\pi n\phi_j/4},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:ghat}
\end{equation}
where $n$ runs over any $N$ consecutive integers (we use the two's-complement range $-N/2\le n<N/2$); both relations are exact on the grid. Since $e^{i\pi n\phi_j/4}=e^{-i\pi n/4}e^{2\pi inj/N}$, the coefficient $\hat g_{q,n}$ is $e^{i\pi n/4}/N$ times the standard FFT of the data. The output is expanded on $C_\mu$ in the same way,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \chi_Q(w_k)&=\sum_\ell\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}\,e^{i\pi\ell\phi_k/4},\\
  \hat\chi_{Q,\ell}&=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\chi_Q(w_k)\,e^{-i\pi\ell\phi_k/4}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:chihat}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The kernel.} Insert \eqref{eq:ghat} for $G_q(z_j)$ (index $n'$) and for $G_{q\pm Q}(z_m)$ (index $n$) into \eqref{eq:cauchy_form}. The momentum average factorizes from the geometry:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \chi_Q(w_k)&=\sum_{n',n}\Big[P^+_Q(n',n)\,K_{n'n}(w_k)\\
  &\qquad\quad+P^-_Q(n',n)\,K_{n'n}(-w_k)\Big],\\
  K_{n'n}(w)&=\sum_{j,m}c_j\,d_m\,\frac{e^{i\pi(n'\phi_j+n\phi_m)/4}}{z_j-z_m+w},\\
  P^\pm_Q(n',n)&=\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\hat g_{q,n'}\hat g_{q\pm Q,n},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the kernel $K_{n'n}$ and the momentum correlations $P^\pm_Q$. By \eqref{eq:doubled}, $-w_k=w_{k+N/2}$, so the second term is the first kernel read at $k+N/2$. Project on the output modes with \eqref{eq:chihat}; for the second term substitute $k'=k+N/2$ and use $\phi_k=\phi_{k'}-4$, which produces $e^{i\pi\ell}=(-1)^\ell$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}&=\sum_{n',n}\widehat K_{n'n\ell}\Big[P^+_Q(n',n)\\
&\qquad\quad+(-1)^\ell P^-_Q(n',n)\Big],\\
P^\pm_Q(n',n)&=\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\hat g_{q,n'}\,\hat g_{q\pm Q,n},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:modes_bubble}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat K_{n'n\ell}={}&\frac1N\sum_ke^{-i\pi\ell\phi_k/4}\\
&\times\sum_{j,m}c_j\,d_m\,\frac{e^{i\pi(n'\phi_j+n\phi_m)/4}}{z_j-z_m+w_k}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
This is the exterior-Cauchy form \eqref{eq:cauchy_form} in another basis, exact term by term. The kernel depends only on the two dumbbells and on $\beta$ (through $c_j$); all of $G$, model and momentum, is in $P^\pm_Q$. For each $k$ the inner double sum is a two-dimensional FFT over $(j,m)$ of the array $c_jd_m/(z_j-z_m+w_k)$, and the outer sum is an FFT over $k$: $O(N^3\log N)$ once, 9\,s at $N=512$ and 86\,s at $N=1024$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:lambda_indep} shows how to make $N$ small).

\paragraph{The mode windows.} In exact arithmetic the sums in \eqref{eq:modes_bubble} run over all $N$ values of $n'$, $n$ and $\ell$. In practice only a window of each index matters, because the coefficients of $G$ and of $\chi$ decay exponentially (Appendix~\ref{sec:decay}) and are rounding noise below $\sim10^{-15}$ of their maximum. The windows used are $n',n\in[-32,159]$, $\ell\in[-32,95]$ for $(\lambda,\mu)=(0.2,0.4)$ at $N=512$, and $n',n\in[-32,319]$, $\ell\in[-32,159]$ for $(0.1,0.2)$ at $N=1024$; in quantics they are embedded in $2^{R_n}$ and $2^{R_\ell}$ slots (Sec.~\ref{sec:tensors}). The grid must resolve the window, $N\ge n_{\max}-n_{\min}$, which with the decay rate \eqref{eq:decay} means $N\gtrsim2\log(1/\varepsilon)/(\alpha\lambda)$.

\subsection{Compressing the kernel}
\label{sec:tucker}

\paragraph{Tucker form and truncated HOSVD.} For a tensor $T_{n'n\ell}$ let $T_{(i)}$ be its mode-$i$ unfolding (the matrix with the $i$-th index as rows and the other two, combined, as columns). The higher-order SVD takes for $U^{(i)}$ the leading left singular vectors of $T_{(i)}$ and forms the core $C=T\times_1U^{(1)\dagger}\times_2U^{(2)\dagger}\times_3U^{(3)\dagger}$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C_{abc}&=\sum_{n'n\ell}T_{n'n\ell}\,\overline{U^{(1)}_{n'a}}\,\overline{U^{(2)}_{nb}}\,\overline{U^{(3)}_{\ell c}},\\
  T_{n'n\ell}&\approx\sum_{a=1}^{r_1}\sum_{b=1}^{r_2}\sum_{c=1}^{r_3}C_{abc}\,U^{(1)}_{n'a}\,U^{(2)}_{nb}\,U^{(3)}_{\ell c}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:tucker}
\end{equation}
Each rank $r_i$ is the smallest with $\sum_{s>r_i}(\sigma^{(i)}_s)^2\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}3\lVert T\rVert_F^2$, $\sigma^{(i)}_s$ the singular values of $T_{(i)}$. Since the three truncations are orthogonal projections, their errors add at most in quadrature, $\lVert T-\hat T\rVert_F^2\le\sum_i\sum_{s>r_i}(\sigma^{(i)}_s)^2\le\varepsilon^2\lVert T\rVert_F^2$, so $\varepsilon$ bounds the relative error of the compressed tensor. The result is quasi-optimal: its error is at most $\sqrt3$ times that of the best Tucker approximation with the same ranks. Applied to the weighted kernel $\widetilde K_{n'n\ell}=s_{n'}s_n\widehat K_{n'n\ell}$ of Appendix~\ref{sec:decay}, whose weights $s_n$ make a relative truncation error of $\widetilde K$ an error of the same size in $\hat\chi$,
\begin{equation}
s_{n'}\,s_n\,\widehat K_{n'n\ell}\approx\sum_{a,b,c}C_{abc}\,U^{(1)}_{n'a}\,U^{(2)}_{nb}\,U^{(3)}_{\ell c},
\label{eq:kernel_tucker}
\end{equation}
at $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ the ranks are $(r_1,r_2,r_3)=(31,33,44)$ for $(\lambda,\mu)=(0.2,0.4)$ and $(56,60,87)$ for $(0.1,0.2)$. The core has $r_1r_2r_3=45\,012$ entries in the example.

\paragraph{The bubble in factorized form.} Insert \eqref{eq:kernel_tucker} into \eqref{eq:modes_bubble} and absorb the weights into the leaves, $\tilde U^{(i)}_{na}=U^{(i)}_{na}/s_n$. This is the formula the network computes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_{q,a}&=\sum_n\hat g_{q,n}\,\tilde U^{(1)}_{na},\qquad B_{q,b}=\sum_n\hat g_{q,n}\,\tilde U^{(2)}_{nb},\\
Y^\pm_c(Q)&=\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\sum_{a,b}A_{q,a}\,C_{abc}\,B_{q\pm Q,b},\\
\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}&=\sum_cU^{(3)}_{\ell c}\Big[Y^+_c(Q)+(-1)^\ell\,Y^-_c(Q)\Big],\\
\chi_Q(w_k)&=\sum_\ell\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}\,e^{i\pi\ell\phi_k/4}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:factorized}
\end{equation}
Per momentum the input is reduced to $r_1+r_2$ numbers; $Y^\pm$ are $r_1r_2$ cyclic correlations in $q$ contracted with the core; the output needs $r_3$ modes. Nothing in \eqref{eq:factorized} depends on $N$ except the final synthesis. For any $G$, the definition of $Y^\pm$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
  Y^-_c(Q)=Y^+_c(-Q\bmod N_q),
  \label{eq:yminus_identity}
\end{equation}
since $B_{q-Q,b}=B_{q+(-Q),b}$. The branch $Y^-$ therefore follows from $Y^+$ by the reflection $Q\to-Q$, without a second contraction (Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}), and since the reflection changes a bond by at most a factor 2, the bonds of the two branches agree within that factor. For the even band $\varepsilon_q=3\cos q$ the two branches are equal: $A_{-q,a}=A_{q,a}$ and $B_{-q,b}=B_{q,b}$, and the substitution $q\to-q$, which maps the momentum grid onto itself, gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac1{N_q}\sum_qA_{q,a}B_{q-Q,b}=\frac1{N_q}\sum_qA_{q,a}B_{q+Q,b},
  \label{eq:reflection}
\end{equation}
that is, $Y^-(Q)=Y^+(Q)$ exactly on the grid. Then $\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}=[1+(-1)^\ell]\sum_cU^{(3)}_{\ell c}Y^+_c(Q)$: the odd modes vanish, which is the evenness $\chi_Q(-w)=\chi_Q(w)$ of the bubble of an even band. Computed by two separate zip-ups, each recompressed at $10^{-9}$, the two trains differ by $\lVert Y^--Y^+\rVert/\lVert Y^+\rVert=1.4\times10^{-11}$. The network does not use this symmetry: it computes both branches, as it must for a band that is not even.

\paragraph{Why the ranks are small.} By \eqref{eq:spectral} the bubble is a trilinear form in $fA_q$, $A_{q+Q}$ and the output kernel $1/(w+\varepsilon-\varepsilon')$, and \eqref{eq:cauchy_form} is its discretization. The first $G$ enters only through $\sum_jc_jG_q(z_j)/(z_j-x)=\int f(\varepsilon)A_q(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon/(\varepsilon-x)$, the Cauchy transform of $fA_q$ at the points $x=z_m-w_k$; the second through the Cauchy transform of $A_{q\pm Q}$ at $z_j\pm w_k$; and the output is the Cauchy transform of a two-particle spectral function on $[-6,6]$, evaluated on $C_\mu$. So each leg of $\widetilde K$ is a Cauchy kernel $1/(x-\varepsilon)$ between a segment and a set at a positive distance from it, with $\Lambda(\mu,\beta)$ the lowest level of the set $[-3,3]\pm C_\mu$ in the dumbbell family of $G$ ($0.40$ for $\mu=0.4$ and $0.23$ for $\mu=0.2$ at $\beta=\pi$):
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{2.3cm}p{2.7cm}p{1.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
leg & \rr source segment & \rr target set & \rr lowest level of the target \\
\midrule
1 ($n'$) & \rr $[-3,3]$, weight $fA$ & \rr $[-3,3]-C_\mu$ (the ``cloud'') & \rr $\Lambda(\mu,\beta)$ \\
2 ($n$) & \rr $[-3,3]$, weight $A$ & \rr $[-3,3]+C_\mu$ (the same set) & \rr $\Lambda(\mu,\beta)$ \\
3 ($\ell$) & \rr $[-6,6]$ (doubled family, level 0) & \rr $C_\mu$ & \rr $\mu$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In dumbbell coordinates a Cauchy kernel from the band to a contour at level $\Lambda$ is the diagonal multiplier $e^{-\alpha\Lambda n}$ of \eqref{eq:multiplier}, so its singular values decay exponentially at the rate $\alpha\Lambda$ and its $\varepsilon$-rank is $\log(1/\varepsilon)/(\alpha\Lambda)$. The discrete Lehmann representation works the same way, with the dumbbell level in the role of $\log\Lambda_{\rm DLR}$. Measured for $\mu$ from 0.2 to 0.8, the ranks follow, within 3\,\%,
\begin{equation}
  r_3\approx0.90\,\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{\alpha\mu},\qquad r_{1,2}\approx0.65\,\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{\alpha\Lambda(\mu,\beta)} ;
  \label{eq:ranklaw}
\end{equation}
for $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ this gives $42$ and $31$ at $\mu=0.4$ (measured 44 and 31), and $84$ and $53$ at $\mu=0.2$ (measured 87 and 56). The prefactors are below 1 because the HOSVD measures the error in the Frobenius norm over the whole cloud, most of which lies far above level $\Lambda$; the sup-norm version (the number of poles needed to represent a Green function on $C_\Lambda$) is $\log(1/\varepsilon)/(\alpha\Lambda)$ within 1 to 3\,\%. The ranks therefore grow like $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ (about $+9$ per two decades for $(0.2,0.4)$ and $+16$ for $(0.1,0.2)$), like $1/\mu$, and roughly linearly with $\beta$ at fixed level, because at fixed level the whole dumbbell family moves toward the real axis as $T\to0$. They do not depend on $N$ or on $\lambda$.

\section{Quantics tensor trains}
\label{sec:qtt}

\paragraph{Bits of an index.} An index $q\in\{0,\ldots,2^R-1\}$ is written in binary,
\begin{equation}
  q=\sum_{i=1}^Rq_i\,2^{R-i},\qquad q_i\in\{0,1\},
  \label{eq:bits}
\end{equation}
with $q_1$ the most significant bit and $q_R$ the least significant one. A vector $v(q)$ of length $2^R$ is thereby a tensor $v(q_1,\ldots,q_R)$ with $R$ indices of dimension $d=2$, its \emph{quantics} form; $d$ is the \emph{physical dimension} of every site. A signed index such as a Fourier mode $n\in[-2^{R-1},2^{R-1})$ is stored in \emph{two's complement}, as the bits of $n\bmod2^R$:
\begin{equation}
  n=-n_1\,2^{R-1}+\sum_{i=2}^Rn_i\,2^{R-i},
  \label{eq:twos}
\end{equation}
so $n_1$ is the sign bit, and the nonnegative modes come first, followed by the negative ones, each $n<0$ at position $2^R+n$. This is the order in which an FFT returns its modes. ``Most significant first'' means the chain carries $q_1,q_2,\ldots,q_R$ in this order; ``least significant first'' the reverse.

\paragraph{Tensor trains and bond dimension.} A tensor train (TT, or matrix-product state, MPS) writes a tensor with $L$ indices as a product of matrices, one per index (\emph{site}),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  v(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_L)=M^{[1]}_{\sigma_1}M^{[2]}_{\sigma_2}\cdots M^{[L]}_{\sigma_L},\\
  M^{[i]}_{\sigma_i}\in\mathbb C^{\chi_{i-1}\times\chi_i},\qquad\chi_0=\chi_L=1 .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:tt}
\end{equation}
The $\chi_i$ are the \emph{bond dimensions} (``bonds''); the train stores $\sum_id\,\chi_{i-1}\chi_i$ numbers instead of $d^L$. A tensor with an additional dense index (the Tucker indices $a$, $b$, $c$ below) is a train whose first or last core carries that index, so that $\chi_0$ or $\chi_L$ equals its dimension. The smallest bond $\chi_i$ that represents the tensor exactly depends only on the tensor and the site order: it is the rank of the \emph{unfolding} matrix whose rows are indexed by $(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_i)$ and whose columns by $(\sigma_{i+1},\ldots,\sigma_L)$. A matrix-product operator (MPO) is the same object for a linear map, $W(\sigma'_1\ldots\sigma'_L;\sigma_1\ldots\sigma_L)=W^{[1]}_{\sigma'_1\sigma_1}\cdots W^{[L]}_{\sigma'_L\sigma_L}$, with cores carrying an input and an output bit. Applying an MPO of bond $D_W$ to a train of bond $\chi$ gives, before any compression, a train of bond $D_W\chi$.

\paragraph{Bit orders.} For a function of two variables the bit strings are concatenated, and the order matters because the bonds are ranks of unfoldings. \emph{Sequential} order puts all bits of one variable before those of the other ($n_1\ldots n_R\,q_1\ldots q_{R_q}$); \emph{interleaved} order alternates them ($q_1n_1q_2n_2\ldots$), possibly with one variable reversed ($q_1n_Rq_2n_{R-1}\ldots$). Bits that interact should sit next to each other. A smooth function of two variables is best sequential: the single matrix coupling them has low rank, and interleaving spreads it over many bonds. A Fourier-type coupling $e^{2\pi i\,nq/2^R}$ is best interleaved with one variable reversed, because the bit $q_i$ couples only to the bits $n_j$ with $i+j>R$ (Appendix~\ref{app:qft}). For this reason the input $\hat g_{q,n}$ of the method is interleaved, with $n$ least significant first, and the output $\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}$ is sequential (Appendix~\ref{sec:choices}).

\paragraph{The adder and the subtractor.} The cyclic correlation $\frac1{N_q}\sum_qA_qB_{q+Q}$ over the momentum grid, $N_q=2^{R_q}$, is the contraction of $A(q)$ and $B(q')$ with the tensor $\delta_{q',\,(q+Q)\bmod N_q}$, which is an MPO of bond dimension 2 over the bits of $q$, $q'$ and $Q$. Binary addition propagates a carry $c\in\{0,1\}$ from the least significant bit upward; the local tensor at bit $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \big[W^{[i]}\big]_{c_{\rm in}c_{\rm out}}(q_i,Q_i,q'_i)={}&\delta\big(q'_i,\ q_i\oplus Q_i\oplus c_{\rm in}\big)\\
  &\times\delta\big(c_{\rm out},\ \lfloor(q_i+Q_i+c_{\rm in})/2\rfloor\big),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:adder}
\end{equation}
with $\oplus$ the sum modulo 2, $c_{\rm in}=0$ at the least significant bit and the carry out of the most significant bit discarded (addition modulo $N_q$: momenta are periodic). The bond index is the carry, so the bond is exactly $D_W=2$ at every site, for any $R_q$ and with no truncation. The subtractor $\delta_{q',\,(q-Q)\bmod N_q}$ has the same form with a borrow, $q'_i=q_i\oplus Q_i\oplus b_{\rm in}$ and $b_{\rm out}=1$ iff $q_i-Q_i-b_{\rm in}<0$; equivalently, it is the adder with the roles of $q$ and $q'$ exchanged ($q=q'+Q$). In the network (Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}) the legs $q_i$ and $q'_i$ are contracted with the two input branches and the legs $Q_i$ remain open, so one contraction gives the correlation for all $N_q$ transfer momenta at once.

\paragraph{The reflection.} The reflection $Q\to-Q\bmod N_q$ (two's-complement negation: complement every bit, then add 1) is also an MPO of bond 2: a bitwise NOT followed by the ``$+1$'' tensor $P[c_{\rm out},{\rm out},{\rm in},c_{\rm in}]=1$ iff ${\rm out}={\rm in}\oplus c_{\rm in}$ and $c_{\rm out}={\rm in}\wedge c_{\rm in}$, with $c_{\rm in}=1$ at the least significant bit and the final carry discarded.

\paragraph{Recompression.} A train produced by an operation (an MPO applied to a train, a sum as in Appendix~\ref{app:sum}, a contraction) in general has bonds larger than needed. A QR sweep followed by a sweep of truncated SVDs brings them down to what the tolerance requires, with the error bound of TT-SVD (Appendix~\ref{app:recomp}). Every intermediate of the contraction is recompressed this way at relative tolerance $10^{-9}$.

\paragraph{Zip-up.} To apply an MPO of bond $D_W$ to a train of bond $\chi$, one can form the product train (bond $D_W\chi$) and recompress it, at a cost $O(Ld\,(D_W\chi)^3)$ that is prohibitive when $D_W\chi$ reaches $10^4$ (it would be $\approx14\,800$ in step \stp4 below). The zip-up contracts and truncates site by site in a single sweep. At site $i$ the carried matrix, whose rows are the truncated bond $\chi'_{i-1}$, is contracted with the $i$-th MPO and train cores, which produces a matrix with $d\chi'_{i-1}$ rows (the truncated bond and the output leg of site $i$) and $D_W\chi$ columns (the right bonds of the MPO and of the train); a truncated SVD factors it, $U$ becomes the finished core $i$, and $SV^\dagger$ is carried to site $i+1$. The product of bond $D_W\chi$ is never formed: the largest matrix has one side of size $d\chi'$ and the other $D_W\chi$ (the largest SVD of the run is $552\times6048$). Since the truncation at site $i$ does not yet know the sites to its right, it is slightly suboptimal; a final recompression sweep removes the excess (the two rows of bonds in Table~\ref{tab:steps}). The same scheme contracts two trains over shared physical legs: in steps \stp1 and \stp2 the leaf train is zipped along $\hat g$ over the legs $n_i$, with an SVD after every $q$ site, and in step \stp4 the adder sits between $A$ and $B'$, the legs $q_i$, $q'_i$ are summed and $Q_i$ stays open.

\section{The Lindhard function}
\label{app:lindhard}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{residues_eq6.pdf}
  \caption{The residue argument behind \eqref{eq:residues_h}, for $\beta=\pi$ and the $h$ of the model with $m=1$. Crosses: the poles $i\omega_n=\pm i,\pm3i,\ldots$ of $f$, where $fh$ has the residues $-h(i\omega_n)/\beta$. Dots: the poles of $h$, $\varepsilon_q$ (here 1.5) and $\varepsilon_{q+Q}-i\nu_m$ (here $-1-2i$). The integral over the circle $C_R$ equals the sum of the integrals over the small circles, i.e.\ $2\pi i$ times all residues inside $C_R$. It tends to zero as $R\to\infty$, so the residues at the crosses and at the dots add up to zero.}
  \label{fig:residues}
\end{figure}

For the model \eqref{eq:model}, $h(z)=G_q(z)\,G_{q+Q}(z+i\nu_m)=1/[(z-\varepsilon_q)(z+i\nu_m-\varepsilon_{q+Q})]$ has only two poles, and the residues at the Matsubara frequencies in \eqref{eq:matsum_residues} can be traded for the residues at these two poles (Fig.~\ref{fig:residues}).

Let $h$ be meromorphic and $O(z^{-2})$ at infinity, with poles $z_p$ none of which is a Matsubara frequency, and let $C_R$ be the circle $\lvert z\rvert=R$, counterclockwise, with $R$ an even multiple of $\pi/\beta$, halfway between two poles of $f$. By the residue theorem, $\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_R}fh\,dz$ is the sum of the residues of $fh$ inside $C_R$: those at the poles $i\omega_n$ of $f$ and those at the poles $z_p$ of $h$. On $C_R$, $\lvert h\rvert\le c/R^2$ and $\lvert f\rvert$ stays bounded ($f\to1$ for $\mathrm{Re}\,z\to-\infty$, $f\to0$ for $\mathrm{Re}\,z\to+\infty$, and $C_R$ passes between the poles), so the integral is at most of order $2\pi R\cdot c/R^2\to0$. As $R\to\infty$ every pole ends up inside, so the two groups of residues add up to zero, and with \eqref{eq:matsum_residues}
\begin{equation}
  \frac1\beta\sum_nh(i\omega_n)=\sum_{\text{poles }z_p\text{ of }h}\Res_{z=z_p}\big[f(z)\,h(z)\big].
  \label{eq:residues_h}
\end{equation}
For example, $h(z)=1/[(z-a)(z-b)]$ has the residues $f(a)/(a-b)$ at $a$ and $f(b)/(b-a)$ at $b$, so
\begin{equation}
  \frac1\beta\sum_n\frac1{(i\omega_n-a)(i\omega_n-b)}=\frac{f(a)-f(b)}{a-b} :
  \label{eq:two_poles}
\end{equation}
an infinite sum, whose terms fall like $1/n^2$, replaced by two terms. With $a=1.5$, $b=-1-2i$ and $\beta=\pi$ the right-hand side is $-0.23163+0.18530\,i$, and the sum over $\lvert n\rvert<10^6$ reaches it to $2\times10^{-7}$. The bubble of the model is this example with $a=\varepsilon_q$ and $b=\varepsilon_{q+Q}-i\nu_m$. With $f(\varepsilon_{q+Q}-i\nu_m)=f(\varepsilon_{q+Q})$ it is the Lindhard function
\begin{equation}
  \chi_Q(i\nu_m)=\frac1{N_q}\sum_q\frac{f(\varepsilon_q)-f(\varepsilon_{q+Q})}{i\nu_m+\varepsilon_q-\varepsilon_{q+Q}} .
  \label{eq:lindhard}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is a rational function of $i\nu_m$; replacing $i\nu_m$ by any $w\notin\mathbb R$ gives the unique analytic continuation of $\chi_Q$ off the real axis (Sec.~\ref{sec:contour}).

\section{Convergence of the dumbbell quadrature}
\label{app:trap}

Let $\Phi$ be 8-periodic and analytic in the strip $\lvert\Imag\phi\rvert<a$, and bounded there by $M$. Shifting the line of integration in the Fourier coefficients $\hat\Phi_n=\frac18\int_{-1}^7\Phi\,e^{-i\pi n\phi/4}d\phi$ to $\Imag\phi=\mp a$ (the vertical sides cancel by periodicity) gives $\lvert\hat\Phi_n\rvert\le Me^{-\pi\lvert n\rvert a/4}$. The $N$-point trapezoidal sum of $\Phi=\sum_n\hat\Phi_ne^{i\pi n\phi/4}$ is exact for $\lvert n\rvert<N$ and aliases the modes $n=pN$ onto the mean, so
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \Delta\phi\sum_j\Phi(\phi_j)-\int_{-1}^{7}\Phi\,d\phi=8\sum_{p\ne0}\hat\Phi_{pN},\\
  \Big\lvert\,\cdot\,\Big\rvert\le\frac{16M\,e^{-\pi Na/4}}{1-e^{-\pi Na/4}} .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:trap_error}
\end{equation}
For the integrands of \eqref{eq:weights}, $\Phi(\phi)=J(\zeta)F(\omega(\zeta))f(\omega(\zeta))$ with $\zeta=\phi+i\tau\lambda$. A complex shift $\phi\to\phi+i\tau\delta$ moves the level from $\lambda$ to $\lambda+\delta$, so $\Phi$ is analytic in the strip $\lvert\Imag\phi\rvert<a$ with $a=\tau d$, where $d$ is the distance in level from $\lambda$ to the nearest singularity: the band (level 0, $d=\lambda$), the poles of $f$ (level 1, $d=1-\lambda$), or a singularity of $F$. With $\alpha=\pi\tau/4$, the error is
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{\rm trap}\approx e^{-\alpha Nd}.
  \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
For the direct formula \eqref{eq:direct} the singularities of $F$ are the shifted cuts, at level distance $\approx\lambda'_{\min}$ (near the band the level is locally proportional to the height, so the level of $z_j\pm w_k$ above the band equals the level distance of $z_j$ from the shifted cut), hence $d=\min(\lambda,\lambda'_{\min})$. The estimate $e^{-\alpha N\min(\lambda,\lambda'_{\min})}$ matches the measured errors: for $\mu=0.2$, $\lambda=0.12$ and $N=2^8$ it gives $e^{-28.6}=4\times10^{-13}$, and $\lambda=0.2$, $N=2^8$ gives $e^{-7.8}=4\times10^{-4}$. The two distances are balanced at $\lambda\approx\mu/2$, so the levels are chosen in that ratio.

\section{Decay of the coefficients and the weighted kernel}
\label{sec:decay}

\paragraph{Strip analyticity and the multiplier.} The function $g(\zeta)=G_q(\omega(\zeta))$ is 8-periodic in $\Real\zeta$ and analytic in the open strip $0<\Imag\zeta<2\tau$, i.e.\ $0<\lambda<2$: $\omega$ maps the strip into $\mathbb C\setminus[-3,3]$, where $G_q$ is analytic, and at the two poles of $\omega$ (at $\zeta=1+i\tau$ and $5+i\tau$) $G_q(\omega)\to0$ is still analytic. The lines $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=2$ are the boundary, where $\omega$ is on the band. The coefficients at level $\lambda$ are $\hat g_n(\lambda)=\frac18\int_{-1}^7g(\phi+i\tau\lambda)e^{-i\pi n\phi/4}d\phi$ (the continuum version of \eqref{eq:ghat}). Shifting the line of integration from height $\tau\lambda_0$ to $\tau\lambda$ inside the strip is allowed by Cauchy's theorem (the vertical sides cancel by periodicity) and gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \hat g_n(\lambda)=e^{-\alpha(\lambda-\lambda_0)n}\,\hat g_n(\lambda_0),\\
  0<\lambda,\lambda_0<2,\qquad\alpha=\frac{\pi\tau}4 .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:multiplier}
\end{equation}
Changing the level is a diagonal operation on the Fourier modes. Because $\hat g_n(\lambda_0)$ stays bounded as $\lambda_0\to0^+$ and as $\lambda_0\to2^-$ (the boundary values of $G$ on the band are bounded or integrable),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \lvert\hat g_n(\lambda)\rvert&\lesssim e^{-\alpha\lambda n}&&(n>0),\\
  \lvert\hat g_n(\lambda)\rvert&\lesssim e^{-\alpha(2-\lambda)\lvert n\rvert}&&(n<0):
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:decay}
\end{equation}
positive modes ``see'' the band at level 0, negative modes its image at level 2. At $\lambda=0.2$ the rates are $0.196$ and $1.77$ per mode, so the coefficients reach $10^{-15}$ of their maximum at $n\approx176$ and $n\approx-20$; smaller coefficients are rounding noise of the double-precision data. The same holds for $\chi$ on $C_\mu$ with $\mu$ in place of $\lambda$, which sets the $\ell$ window.

\paragraph{The weights.} The worst case among all admissible $G$ is a pole on the band, whose coefficients saturate \eqref{eq:decay}. Define
\begin{equation}
  s_n=\begin{cases}\max\big(e^{-\alpha\lambda n},\,10^{-15}\big),&n\ge0,\\[2pt]
  \max\big(e^{-\alpha(2-\lambda)\lvert n\rvert},\,10^{-15}\big),&n<0,\end{cases}
  \label{eq:sn}
\end{equation}
and write the bubble as $\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}=\sum_{n'n}\widetilde K_{n'n\ell}\widetilde P^\pm_Q(n',n)$ with the \emph{weighted kernel} $\widetilde K_{n'n\ell}=s_{n'}s_n\widehat K_{n'n\ell}$ and $\widetilde P=P/(s_{n'}s_n)$. For every $G$ whose coefficients are bounded by the worst case, $\lvert\hat g_{q,n}\rvert\le c\,s_n$, the entries of $\widetilde P$ are bounded by $c^2$, so an error $\delta\widetilde K$ of the weighted kernel gives $\lvert\delta\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}\rvert\le c^2\sum_{n'n}\lvert\delta\widetilde K_{n'n\ell}\rvert$. A relative truncation error $\varepsilon$ of $\widetilde K$ is therefore directly an error $\approx\varepsilon$ of $\hat\chi$, uniformly over the admissible $G$. Compressing the unweighted $\widehat K$ would spend rank on the high modes, which never carry signal, and would give no error control. The floor $10^{-15}$ is needed because below it the data are noise: without it, $1/s_n$ reaches $10^{25}$, the noise is amplified, and the compressed kernel gives errors of $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-5}$ at any tolerance.

\paragraph{The weighted kernel does not depend on $\lambda$.}
\label{sec:lambda_indep}
By \eqref{eq:multiplier}, the ratios $a_{q,n}\equiv\hat g_{q,n}(\lambda)/s_n(\lambda)$ are the coefficients at level 0 for $n\ge0$ and at level 2 for $n<0$ (the floor aside): level-free Laurent coefficients of $G_q$, independent of $\lambda$. The bubble is $\hat\chi_{Q,\ell}=\sum_{n'n}\widetilde K_{n'n\ell}\frac1{N_q}\sum_q[a_{q,n'}a_{q+Q,n}+(-1)^\ell a_{q,n'}a_{q-Q,n}]$, and neither $\hat\chi$ (a contour integral, independent of the contour) nor the $a$'s depend on $\lambda$. Hence $\widetilde K$ is the same trilinear form for every forward-safe $\lambda$, up to the quadrature error $e^{-\alpha N(\lambda'_{\min}-\lambda)}$. Measured at $\mu=0.4$, the weighted kernels built at $\lambda=0.2$ and $\lambda=0.1$ (both $N=512$) differ by $1.7\times10^{-11}$ of their maximum. Consequently the ranks of the compressed kernel depend on $\mu$, $\beta$ and $\varepsilon$ only, not on $\lambda$ or $N$, and the kernel can be built at the cheapest $(\lambda,N)$ and applied to data at another $\lambda$. A small $\lambda$ makes the margin $\lambda'_{\min}-\lambda$ large, so $N$ is set by the window alone, with the window cut at the target $\varepsilon$. For $\mu=0.4$ this means $\lambda=0.1$, $N=256$ and 2\,s instead of 10\,s; for $\mu=0.2$, $\lambda=0.05$, $N=512$ and 29\,s plus 19\,s of HOSVD instead of 108\,s plus 120\,s, with identical ranks and errors.

\section{TT-SVD and the truncation criterion}
\label{app:ttsvd}

Any tensor can be brought into the form \eqref{eq:tt} by successive SVDs: reshape $v$ into the $d\times d^{L-1}$ matrix $v_{(1)}$, compute $v_{(1)}=USV^\dagger$, take $M^{[1]}=U$ (its columns are the first bond), reshape $SV^\dagger$ into a $(d\chi_1)\times d^{L-2}$ matrix, and continue. At each bond the singular values are truncated by the \emph{relative Frobenius criterion}: keep the smallest $\chi_i$ with
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{s>\chi_i}\sigma_s^2\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{L-1}\,\lVert v\rVert^2,
  \label{eq:trunc}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ each bond discards at most the fraction $\varepsilon^2/(L-1)$ of the squared norm. The truncations at different bonds are orthogonal projections, so the discarded weights add and the relative error in Frobenius norm is at most $\varepsilon$; the result is within a factor $\sqrt{L-1}$ of the best train with the same bonds. All tolerances quoted for trains are relative tolerances $\varepsilon$ in this sense. The ``bond'' or ``rank'' of an object in the tables is the bond dimension it needs at the stated $\varepsilon$.

\section{The discrete Fourier transform as an MPO}
\label{app:qft}

For $k=\sum_ik_i2^{R-i}$ and $\ell=\sum_j\ell_j2^{R-j}$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  e^{2\pi ik\ell/2^R}&=\prod_{i,j}\exp\big(2\pi i\,k_i\ell_j\,2^{R-i-j}\big)\\
  &=\prod_{i+j>R}\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi i\,k_i\ell_j}{2^{\,i+j-R}}\Big),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:dft_bits}
\end{equation}
because the factors with $i+j\le R$ equal 1. The most significant bit $k_1$ therefore couples only to the least significant bit $\ell_R$, $k_2$ to $\ell_{R-1}$ and $\ell_R$, and so on. If site $i$ of the MPO carries the pair $(k_i,\ell_{R+1-i})$, i.e.\ one index in reversed bit order, then $k_i$ couples to the pairs at sites $i'\le i$ with the phase $2\pi/2^{\,i-i'+1}$, which decreases geometrically with the distance along the chain and is negligible beyond a distance of about $\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$. The interaction is quasi-local and the MPO has bond dimension $O(\log(1/\varepsilon))$. This is the quantum-Fourier-transform circuit, and the reason the QFT returns the modes in bit-reversed order. With both indices in the same order the strongest coupling, $k_1\ell_R$, joins the two ends of the chain and the bond is maximal. The inverse transform of the method, $F(k,\ell)=e^{i\pi\ell\phi_k/4}=e^{-i\pi\ell/4}e^{2\pi i\ell k/N}$, is a phase times \eqref{eq:dft_bits}; with $\ell$ most significant first and $k$ least significant first its MPO has bonds $2,4,8,11,11,8,4,2$ at tolerance $10^{-12}$, so its bond dimension is $D=11$.

\section{Sum of two trains}
\label{app:sum}

The sum $v+v'$ of two trains is the train with cores
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  M^{[1]}_{\sigma}\oplus M'^{[1]}_{\sigma}&=\big(M^{[1]}_\sigma\ \ M'^{[1]}_\sigma\big),\\
  M^{[L]}_\sigma\oplus M'^{[L]}_\sigma&=\begin{pmatrix}M^{[L]}_\sigma\\M'^{[L]}_\sigma\end{pmatrix},\\
  M^{[i]}_\sigma\oplus M'^{[i]}_\sigma&=\begin{pmatrix}M^{[i]}_\sigma&0\\0&M'^{[i]}_\sigma\end{pmatrix}\quad(1<i<L),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:sum}
\end{equation}
a direct sum: the bonds add, $\chi_i+\chi'_i$, and are brought back down by recompression. A factor that acts on one site, such as the sign $(-1)^\ell=(-1)^{\ell_R}$ (a diagonal tensor on the least significant bit of $\ell$), is multiplied into the corresponding core before the sum.

\section{Recompression}
\label{app:recomp}

A train is recompressed in two sweeps. (i)~A QR sweep from left to right: reshape the first core into a $(d\chi_0)\times\chi_1$ matrix, factor it as $M^{[1]}=QR$ with $Q$ having orthonormal columns, keep $Q$ as the new core and multiply $R$ into the core to its right; repeat up to the last site. Now every core but the last is left-orthogonal, and the norm of the train sits in the last core. (ii)~A sweep from right to left with truncated SVDs: factor the current core (reshaped to $\chi_{i-1}\times(d\chi_i)$) as $USV^\dagger$, truncate by \eqref{eq:trunc}, keep $V^\dagger$ as the new core and multiply $US$ into the core to its left. Because the rest of the train is orthogonal, the singular values of the local matrix are those of the full unfolding at that bond, so each truncation is optimal for its bond, and with \eqref{eq:trunc} the total relative error is at most $\varepsilon$, as for TT-SVD. The cost is $O(Ld\chi^3)$ for a train of bond $\chi$.

\section{Design choices}
\label{sec:choices}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Truncate the raw $\hat g$ and put the weight $1/s_n$ into the leaves. The raw coefficients decay as in \eqref{eq:decay}, and truncating them at $10^{-6}$ still gives a bubble accurate to $10^{-9}$: the leaves divided by $s_n$ lie in the range of the unweighted kernel, which is bounded, so errors in the high modes are not amplified. The weighted array $\hat g/s$ has full rank, bond 256 at $N_q=1024$. In it every mode of the window has the same size, and for each momentum $\hat g_n/s_n$ is close to a plane wave $e^{-i\pi n\phi(\varepsilon_q)/4}$ whose frequency moves nonlinearly with $q$.
  \item Interleave the bits of the input and keep the output sequential (Sec.~\ref{sec:qtt}, bit orders). For $\hat g$, interleaving $q$ and $n$ with $n$ least significant first gives bond 57 at $10^{-6}$; all $n$ bits first gives 76, and all $q$ bits first 149. For the output, frequency bits first and then $Q$ gives 25 to 32, while interleaving gives 36 to 63 for $\hat\chi$ and 48 to 83 for $\chi$ (at $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-8}$). The contraction delivers the output in the best order by construction.
  \item Contract the core into $B$ before the adder. Correlating $A$ and $B$ first creates $M(Q;a,b)$, with a front leg of 1023; its zip-up swells to bonds of 600 to 2600, and capping them costs 3 to 13\,\% accuracy.
  \item Put $\ell$ on the same bits as $k$. The inverse DFT is then the square, QFT-type transform \eqref{eq:dft_bits}, with bond at most 11. Keeping only the 128 window modes on 7 bits gives a rectangular map with bond 32.
  \item Compute $Y^-$ by reflecting $Y^+$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}): one zip-up instead of two, 35\,s instead of 67\,s.
  \item Choose $\lambda\lesssim\mu/2$, so that the copies of Fig.~\ref{fig:dumbbells}(b) stay outside $C_\lambda$ (the three conditions of Sec.~\ref{sec:cauchy}), and set the tolerances to $10^{-8}$ for the kernel, $10^{-6}$ for the input, $10^{-10}$ for the leaves, $10^{-12}$ for the DFT and $10^{-9}$ for the recompression. The kernel ranks grow like $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ and like $1/\mu$ (and with $\beta$), as in \eqref{eq:ranklaw}. They do not grow with $N$ or $\lambda$, and the kernel may be built at a smaller $N$ than the data's (Appendix~\ref{sec:decay}).
\end{enumerate}
